The rationale they give for copper beating oil: their TIPS index has ~4.5 years duration, and at that duration the broad business cycle (which copper proxies) dominates over the direct headline-inflation/oil price impact, which matters more at the short end of the curve.

So to literally replicate the best version: long TIPS index / short nominal index, position on only when copper > its 55dma (lagged one day), and measure IR.

The conceptual framework around it
These aren't mechanical rules but they're the reasoning that motivates the trade:

TIPS behave like duration products. Real rates peak at the same time as nominal rates, so TIPS should be traded like nominal rates. The implication: switching from nominals into real rates during an inflation spike does not fully protect you — losses are smaller than in nominals but still real (2022 is the example, where TIPS lost money once the Fed hiked, except at the very short end).

For genuine inflation protection, trade breakevens, not real rates. Breakevens can generate alpha independent of your general US rates view. Their breakeven cycle playbook:

Breakevens collapse into deep recessions, then rebound aggressively once markets sense the bottom.
Outside deep recessions, they rise well before the first hike and into the early hiking cycle, then fall once the Fed hikes in earnest.
In easing cycles heading toward a soft landing (2024), they widen.
Linker illiquidity is a feature, not a bug, for breakeven trades. In severe stress, real rates sell off (illiquidity) exactly when nominals rally, which artificially depresses breakevens (this drove the breakeven collapses into the 2008 and 2020 Fed cuts). So if you're positioned for breakevens to fall in a hard-landing cutting cycle, a rising illiquidity premium amplifies the trade in your favor.

The drivers underneath breakevens are oil and copper — oil because linkers track headline (not core) inflation, and copper because breakevens ultimately track the business cycle. This is what justifies using those two as the trading signals in the concrete rule above.
